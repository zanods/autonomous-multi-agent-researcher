High-TDP laptop cooling: sample knowledge-base doc

Gaming laptops with high-TDP processors (55W and above) rely on advanced
cooling to sustain boost clocks. The most effective designs combine vapor
chamber cold plates with dual high-static-pressure fans. A vapor chamber
spreads heat laterally across the heatsink far better than traditional
copper heat pipes, reducing hotspot temperatures by 5 to 10 degrees
Celsius under sustained load.

Fan curves matter as much as the heatsink. Aggressive curves hold higher
clocks but produce significant noise, often above 50 dBA. Balanced profiles
trade roughly 5 percent performance for much quieter operation. Undervolting
the CPU by 50 to 80 millivolts is a common companion tweak: it lowers heat
output without reducing clock speeds, letting the cooling system sustain
boost longer.

Liquid metal thermal interface material offers another 3 to 7 degrees of
improvement over stock paste, but it is electrically conductive and risky
to apply. For most users, a quality conventional repaste plus a raised
rear stand for airflow gives the best risk-adjusted gains.
